<p><em>If your team spends half their shift answering "what time do you close?" and "do you have a table for four on Friday?" — there is a better way. Here's what a 24/7 virtual assistant looks like for a real restaurant, and why the same idea works for almost any service business.</em></p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The problem every restaurant owner already knows</h2>

<p>Walk into any busy restaurant at 7pm and watch the host stand for ten minutes. You'll see the same scene play out over and over: the phone rings during a rush, a guest needs to be seated, the host has to choose. Most of the time the phone loses.</p>

<p>Every missed call is a coin flip on revenue. Some callers leave a voicemail. Most just hang up and try the place down the street.</p>

<p>And it's not only the calls. It's the WhatsApp messages at 11pm asking "are you open Sunday?" The Instagram DMs asking "is the salmon gluten-free?" The walk-in who wanted to cancel a Friday booking but couldn't get through. Every one of those is either a lost guest or unpaid work for someone on your team.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What this changes</h2>

<p>Imagine a conversational assistant that lives wherever your guests already are — a chat window on your website, a WhatsApp message, a phone call, a link in your Instagram bio. The guest picks the channel that feels natural to them; the assistant is the same on all of them. Always staffed, always polite, and never having a bad day. It can:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Take a reservation</strong> — checks the calendar, picks the right table for the party size, confirms it instantly.</li>

<li><strong>Reschedule or cancel</strong> — the guest doesn't need to "get through to someone." It just happens.</li>

<li><strong>Answer the daily questions</strong> — opening hours, dress code, parking, what's vegetarian, which dishes contain nuts, whether you accept walk-ins. Always the right answer, never made up.</li>

<li><strong>Take a complaint properly</strong> — logs the details, asks for the date, severity, what went wrong. No more lost feedback because the manager was busy.</li>

<li><strong>Speak the guest's language</strong> — if a tourist writes in French or German, the assistant answers in their language without you needing to translate your menu six times.</li>

<li><strong>Hand off to a human when it should</strong> — if the guest needs something the assistant can't handle, it offers your phone number directly, or texts a member of staff to call them back.</li>
</ul>

<p>The honest framing for owners is this: it's not "AI replacing staff." It's removing the most boring, most repetitive minute-by-minute interruptions so the people you already pay can focus on guests in the room.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What it looks like for your guests</h2>

<p>A guest opens your website on a Tuesday at 10:47pm and types:</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p>"Hi, do you have a table for two tomorrow at 9pm? Anything quiet — it's an anniversary."</p>
</blockquote>

<p>Within seconds, the assistant has confirmed the booking, suggested a quieter corner table, and asked if there are any allergies it should flag for the kitchen. The guest goes to bed happy. You wake up Wednesday morning with a confirmed reservation that — without the assistant — you would have lost completely.</p>

<p>Now imagine that same scene at lunch service the next day. A regular sends a WhatsApp message asking to move their Friday booking from 8 to 9. Instead of pulling your manager away from a full dining room, the assistant handles it, the calendar updates itself, and the guest gets a confirmation in 20 seconds.</p>

<p>That's the whole product, in two scenes.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Built with care for your guests' privacy</h2>

<p>Two questions every owner asks, in order:</p>

<p><strong>"Can it accidentally tell a customer something it shouldn't?"</strong> No. Every reply passes through a privacy filter before it reaches the guest. Other customers' phone numbers, emails, and personal details are blocked automatically. The assistant only knows what's in your knowledge base — and if it doesn't know, it says so plainly instead of inventing an answer.</p>

<p><strong>"Can someone book or cancel using a stranger's number?"</strong> No. Before changing or cancelling a reservation, the assistant asks the guest to prove the phone is theirs with a one-time code — the same way your bank confirms a login. New bookings still flow easily; only changes to an existing reservation need the extra step.</p>

<p>And nothing the assistant tells a guest is "winged." It only answers from a knowledge base <em>you</em> control. Updating the menu, the hours, or a policy is as simple as editing one document — the next conversation uses the new content.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">It learns from your taste</h2>

<p>Every assistant has good days and bad days. The difference here is that <em>you</em> get to coach it.</p>

<p>There's a simple review screen where you (or your manager) can flag a conversation as "good — keep doing this" or "bad — never reply like that again," with a one-line reason. Click a button, and from that point forward the assistant uses your examples as guidance. Over a few weeks of light review, the assistant ends up sounding like <em>your</em> restaurant — your voice, your standards, your judgement calls.</p>

<p>That feedback loop is the part most "AI chatbot" products get wrong. They feel generic forever. This one keeps getting closer to the way you'd answer the question yourself.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What it doesn't do — on purpose</h2>

<p>A few things are intentionally outside the scope, so you know what you'd still handle yourself:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>It doesn't take payments.</strong> Bookings yes, money no. Pre-authorisations and deposits stay with your existing payment processor.</li>

<li><strong>It doesn't replace your team.</strong> When a conversation needs a human — a complicated complaint, a special request, a regular asking for a favour — it hands off cleanly with full context.</li>

<li><strong>It doesn't answer questions you haven't taught it.</strong> If the guest asks something not in your knowledge base, it says "let me put you in touch with the team" rather than guessing.</li>
</ul>

<p>Those boundaries are by design. The product is a sharp tool for the boring 80% of customer conversations, leaving the interesting 20% to the people who actually run your floor.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Getting started</h2>

<p>The setup for an existing restaurant is short and mostly content work, not technical work:</p>

<ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Connect your reservation system.</strong> Whatever you already use to track tables and bookings becomes the source of truth. The assistant reads from and writes to it.</li>

<li><strong>Hand over your knowledge base.</strong> Menu, allergens, opening hours, dress code, cancellation policy, parking notes, anything you'd want a guest to be able to ask. We help you organise it on day one; you maintain it from there.</li>

<li><strong>Decide where the chat window lives.</strong> Your website is the obvious first home. WhatsApp, Instagram, and a dedicated short link are easy add-ons.</li>

<li><strong>Pick the languages you serve.</strong> The assistant ships speaking English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, and Italian. You write the menu once, in one language; it handles the rest.</li>

<li><strong>Run it for a week, then review.</strong> Read the conversations, mark the good ones and the bad ones, click rebuild. The assistant learns your taste.</li>
</ol>

<p>Most restaurants are live within a few days. The slowest part is usually you giving us your real menu in a clean, edit-friendly format — and even that is a one-time cost.</p>
